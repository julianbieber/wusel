# Traders moving goods and money between cities.
#
# Run with:  just drive run scenarios/traders_move_goods.txt
#
# **What this proves that a unit test cannot.** The market arithmetic is all ordinary
# unit tests and the whole-world run in `growth.rs` measures the economy — but neither
# can say that a wagon exists in the running game, that it is drawn, or that it is on
# the road rather than beside it. The reading that settles it is `observe traders`,
# which reports every wagon's tile *and the kind of ground under it*: a travelling
# caravan must be standing on `Road` and a resting one on `Town`, and that is a
# sentence about the real world rather than about the model of it.
#
# The capture is the other half and deliberately the weaker one. A caravan is a
# coloured dot; a PNG can show you it is there and cannot tell you what it carries or
# whether it meant to go where it is going.

# Clocks frozen through the waits, so two runs of this file are comparable. Generation,
# the plan and the bakes are polled per frame and so still land — see the comment in
# snow_falls_and_melts.txt for why that is not a formality.
fixed-delta 0

enter gameplay
wait terrain
wait plan

# **`wait plan` is one frame short of the traders and that is the whole reason this
# condition exists.** The wagons go out on the first frame after the plan is done *and*
# the cities have been seeded, so a scenario that read `observe traders` here would get
# an empty list and assert nothing at all.
wait traders

# A wgsl error draws nothing rather than drawing wrong, and every capture below would
# still be written. `observe log` drains, so this has to come first — it is the only
# read that can see startup.
observe log

# The opening state. Every wagon is resting in a city with an empty hold, every trader
# has its opening purse, and every city has a treasury sized on its town.
observe traders

# And the market before anyone has traded in it: the `prices` column is what a caravan
# compares, so a later diff of this is a diff of the numbers the decisions were taken
# on rather than of their consequences.
observe cities

# Let the world run. 800 frames at a whole second of delta is 1600 economy steps at
# `step_seconds` 0.5 — the budget gh-7 needed after it divided `growth_rate` and
# `claims_per_step` by eight, and eight harvests at `harvest_interval_steps` 200. Two
# steps a frame is well under `max_steps_per_frame`, so nothing is dropped.
fixed-delta 1
step 800

# **The measurement.** Every wagon reports its tile, the ground under it, what it is
# carrying and what it paid. The assertions this file exists for:
#
#   - every caravan whose errand is `travelling` has `"on": "Road"`;
#   - every caravan whose errand is `resting` has `"on": "Town"`;
#   - some caravan is carrying something it paid for, which is what says the market
#     found a margin rather than that the wagons are simply driving about.
observe traders

# The consequence, in the cities. Treasuries have moved in both directions — a city
# that sold has more than it opened with and one that bought has less — and the stocks
# of the seam goods are no longer confined to the fifth of cities that can mine them.
observe cities

# Now go and look. Midday first, because the clock never stops and 1600 steps of
# stepping lands wherever it lands — the first cut of this file captured a midnight
# frame and showed nothing at all. `sun` sets where the planet is *now*, so it has to
# come after the stepping and not before it.
sun 0.5

# The camera opens at the world centre; this is city 46, a borough with roads leaving
# it in three directions. The pan is frame-counted, which is exact under a fixed delta:
# `hold` moves 1.0667 tiles a frame at 1/60.
fixed-delta 1/60
hold a 275
hold s 159
observe camera

# **Read the wagons again immediately before each capture**, because the clock never
# stops: the pan above takes 434 frames, which is seven seconds, in which a wagon covers
# eighty tiles. Whatever was near this city when the numbers above were taken has long
# since left. This reading is what says which wagons were actually in frame — without
# it the capture is a lucky photograph and the file cannot tell you whether it caught
# anything.
observe traders

# Zoomed in two steps, because a wagon is `caravan_sprite_tiles` across — 16 world
# pixels, two tiles — and at gameplay zoom that is a dot rather than a cart. The town,
# the road running into it and the fields around both are in frame at this scale, and a
# wagon resting in the city sits exactly on its centre.
zoom 2
step 2
capture /tmp/wusel-traders/city.png

# And the wide shot, two steps the other way from gameplay zoom, where the point is the
# *network* rather than the wagon: half a dozen cities, the roads between them and
# whatever traffic is on them. A wagon is `caravan_min_screen_px` here — the floor that
# stops it vanishing as the world shrinks, the same trick `city_panel.rs` plays with its
# pick target — so it reads as a coloured speck on a road and not as a cart.
zoom -2
observe traders
step 2
capture /tmp/wusel-traders/roads.png

observe log
